# Stress Management & Mental Wellness

## Understanding Stress

Stress is your body's response to any demand or pressure. While some stress can be motivating (eustress), chronic stress can harm your health.

**Common stress symptoms:**
- Physical: headaches, muscle tension, fatigue, sleep problems
- Emotional: anxiety, irritability, sadness, feeling overwhelmed
- Behavioral: changes in eating/sleeping habits, social withdrawal
- Cognitive: difficulty concentrating, forgetfulness, indecisiveness

## Breathing Techniques for Immediate Relief

### 4-7-8 Breathing
1. Inhale for 4 seconds
2. Hold for 7 seconds
3. Exhale for 8 seconds
4. Repeat 4 times

### Box Breathing (Navy SEAL technique)
1. Inhale for 4 seconds
2. Hold for 4 seconds
3. Exhale for 4 seconds
4. Hold for 4 seconds
5. Repeat 4-6 cycles

### Diaphragmatic Breathing
- Place one hand on chest, one on belly
- Breathe so that only the belly hand moves
- Inhale slowly through nose, exhale through pursed lips
- Practice for 5-10 minutes daily

## Mindfulness Meditation

Mindfulness involves paying attention to the present moment without judgment.

**Getting started:**
1. Find a quiet, comfortable place
2. Set a timer for 5-10 minutes
3. Focus on your breath
4. When your mind wanders, gently return focus to breath
5. Practice daily for best results

**Types of mindfulness practice:**
- Breath awareness meditation
- Body scan meditation
- Walking meditation
- Mindful eating
- Loving-kindness meditation

**Proven benefits:**
- Reduces cortisol (stress hormone) levels
- Improves emotional regulation
- Decreases anxiety and depression symptoms
- Improves focus and attention
- Enhances immune function

## Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)

PMR involves systematically tensing and releasing muscle groups.

**Procedure:**
1. Find a quiet place and lie down
2. Starting with feet, tense muscles for 5-10 seconds
3. Release and feel the difference for 20-30 seconds
4. Move progressively up the body
5. End with facial muscles

**Muscle groups to address:**
- Feet and calves
- Thighs and buttocks
- Abdomen
- Chest and back
- Arms and hands
- Shoulders
- Neck
- Face

## Sleep and Stress Recovery

Poor sleep increases stress, and stress worsens sleep — a harmful cycle.

**Sleep hygiene practices:**
- Maintain consistent sleep/wake times (even weekends)
- Keep bedroom dark, cool (65-68°F), and quiet
- Avoid screens 1 hour before bed (blue light disrupts melatonin)
- Avoid caffeine after 2pm
- Limit alcohol (disrupts REM sleep)
- Create a relaxing bedtime routine

**Sleep duration guidelines:**
- Adults: 7-9 hours per night
- Teenagers: 8-10 hours
- Children: 9-12 hours

**Signs of sleep deprivation:**
- Difficulty concentrating
- Irritability and mood changes
- Impaired decision-making
- Increased stress reactivity
- Weight gain tendencies

## Physical Exercise and Stress

Exercise is one of the most effective stress relievers available.

**Why exercise helps:**
- Releases endorphins (natural mood elevators)
- Reduces cortisol and adrenaline
- Improves sleep quality
- Builds confidence and resilience
- Provides healthy social interaction

**Recommended exercise for stress:**
- Aerobic exercise: 150 minutes/week of moderate intensity
- Strength training: 2-3 sessions per week
- Yoga: combines physical movement with mindfulness
- Walking: even 10-minute walks reduce stress acutely

## Cognitive Strategies

### Cognitive Reframing
Change how you think about stressors.

**Examples:**
- "This is catastrophic" → "This is challenging but manageable"
- "I always fail" → "I struggle with this specific thing"
- "Everything is going wrong" → "This particular situation is difficult"

### Problem-Solving Approach
1. Define the problem clearly
2. Generate possible solutions (brainstorm)
3. Evaluate each solution
4. Choose and implement the best option
5. Evaluate the results

### Worry Time
Schedule a specific 20-30 minute period each day for worrying. When worries arise outside this time, defer them to your worry period.

## Social Support and Connection

**Maintaining social connections:**
- Schedule regular time with friends and family
- Join groups or clubs aligned with interests
- Practice active listening in relationships
- Seek and provide support without judgment
- Limit time with consistently negative people

## Professional Help

Know when to seek professional support.

**Consider professional help when:**
- Stress interferes with daily functioning for 2+ weeks
- You experience panic attacks
- You're using substances to cope
- You have thoughts of self-harm
- Sleep is severely impacted long-term
- Relationships are significantly affected

**Types of professional support:**
- Psychologist or therapist (talk therapy, CBT)
- Psychiatrist (medication management)
- Counselor or life coach
- Employee Assistance Programs (EAP)
- Support groups

**Evidence-based therapies:**
- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
- Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)
- Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR)
- EMDR (for trauma)

## Building Stress Resilience

Resilience is the ability to adapt to stress and adversity.

**Resilience-building practices:**
- Cultivate a growth mindset
- Maintain perspective (will this matter in 5 years?)
- Build strong social networks before you need them
- Develop multiple areas of meaning and purpose
- Practice gratitude (3 things daily)
- Accept that some things are outside your control
- Learn from setbacks rather than ruminating

## Daily Wellness Habits

**Morning routine:**
- Avoid checking phone first thing
- Move your body (even 5-10 minutes)
- Eat a nutritious breakfast
- Set intentions for the day

**Throughout the day:**
- Take regular breaks (every 90 minutes)
- Stay hydrated (2-3 liters water)
- Limit caffeine intake
- Practice brief mindfulness moments

**Evening routine:**
- Review what went well today
- Plan tomorrow's priorities
- Wind down with relaxing activities
- Prepare for quality sleep
